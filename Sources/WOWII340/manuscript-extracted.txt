Double brooms refute Graﬃti.pc Conjecture 340
on total domination in trees
Jonas J. Gebendorfer
17 October 2026
Abstract
Conjecture 340 of DeLaviña’s collection Written on the Wall II asserts that every tree T of
order n > 2 satisﬁes γt(T ) ≤ c(⟨N (L) ∪ L⟩) + mode min(T ) γ(T ), where L is the set of leaves,
c counts components, and modemin(T ) is the smallest most frequent vertex degree. We show
that the conjecture is false. The double broom obtained from a path on twelve vertices by
attaching a leaves to one end and b leaves to the other, with a, b ≥ 2 and a + b ≥ 10, has
total domination number 8 while the right-hand side equals 7. This gives counterexamples of
every order n ≥ 22. A one-ended broom family shows that the additive deﬁcit is unbounded,
and that no inequality γt ≤ c(⟨N (L) ∪ L⟩) + αγ + C with a constant α < 3/ 2 can hold even
among trees whose smallest mode is one, whereas α = 2 is valid. All exact values are proved by
explicit sets and disjoint-neighbourhood packings. An exhaustive census of all 9 114 283 trees
of orders 3 through 22 shows that 22 is the smallest order of a counterexample and that the
four double brooms of that order are the only ones. A standard-library supplement reproduces
every ﬁnite statement.
Keywords. total domination; domination; trees; degree mode; Graﬃti.pc; counterexample.

1 Introduction
Graﬃti.pc is DeLaviña’s conjecture-generating program, a variant of Fajtlowicz’s Graﬃti [ 4]. Its
conjectures are collected in Written on the Wall II (WOWII) [ 5]. A block of entries dated 18
February 2009 concerns the total domination number of trees; several of them are discussed in
[6], and many have since been proved or refuted. Entry 340 is an upper bound. It combines the
ordinary domination number with two simple structural quantities: the number of components of
the subgraph induced by the leaves and their neighbours, and the smallest mode of the degree
sequence.
The general inequality γt(G) ≤ 2γ(G) for graphs without isolated vertices (Lemma 3.2) already
gives an upper bound of the same shape with coeﬃcient two. Conjecture 340 asks for much more
when leaves are the most frequent degree: the coeﬃcient of γ then drops to one. We show that
this is false. The obstruction is elementary. A long path has γt/γ close to 3/ 2, and a single
bundle of leaves at each end of the path makes degree one the mode without creating any further
leaf–support component.
Results. Theorem 4.3 gives counterexamples of every order n ≥ 22, with fully hand-checkable
certiﬁcates. Theorem 5.3 gives a second, one-ended family of counterexamples of every order
n ≥ 23. Theorem 5.5 shows that the additive deﬁcit is unbounded. Theorem 5.6 shows that,

2 Deﬁnitions and the source statement
All graphs are ﬁnite, simple and undirected. For a vertex v, N (v) is its open neighbourhood and
N [v] = N (v) ∪ {v} its closed neighbourhood; for a set X, N (X) = ∪
x∈ X N (x). The subgraph
induced by X is ⟨X⟩, and c(H) is the number of connected components of a graph H. A leaf is a
vertex of degree one, and a support vertex is a vertex adjacent to a leaf. We write L = L(T ) and
S = S(T ) for the sets of leaves and support vertices of a tree T .
A set D ⊆ V (G) is dominating if D ∩ N [v] ̸= ∅ for every vertex v, and total dominating if
D ∩ N (v) ̸= ∅ for every vertex v, including the vertices of D itself. Their minimum sizes are the
domination number γ(G) and the total domination number γt(G); the latter exists when G has
no isolated vertex [ 3, 9]. The mode of the degree sequence is a degree of maximum frequency, and
modemin(G) is the smallest such degree.
The conjecture. The archived WOWII list [ 5] contains the following entry, with status marker
O (open):
340. If T is a tree on n > 2 vertices, then γt ≤ number of components of ⟨N (L) ∪ L⟩+
modemin(T ) ·γ(T ), where L is the set of leaves of T . (dated Feb. 18, 2009)
The entry links WOWII deﬁnitions 94 (total domination number), 100 (induced subgraph), 45
(mode; “in case there is more than one mode, modemin is the smallest mode”) and 49 (domination
number); deﬁnition 46 deﬁnes N (S) as the set of vertices adjacent to at least one vertex of S. In
symbols, the conjecture states
γt(T ) ≤ c(⟨N (L) ∪ L⟩) + modemin(T ) γ(T ) for every tree T of order n > 2. (1)
We write R(T ) for the right-hand side of ( 1).
Lemma 2.1. If T is a tree of order n > 2, then N (L) = S and N (L) ∪ L = S ∪ L. The same set
arises if N (L) is read as the closed neighbourhood N [L].
Proof. A vertex lies in N (L) exactly when it is adjacent to a leaf, that is, when it is a support
vertex; so N (L) = S. Moreover N [L] = N (L) ∪ L. (Since two adjacent leaves would form a
component K2, which a tree of order n > 2 cannot have, S and L are even disjoint.)
Thus the statement has no typographic ambiguity that aﬀects the constructions below.
3 Three elementary lower and upper bounds
Lemma 3.1. Let G be a graph and v1, . . . , v r distinct vertices.
1. If N (v1), . . . , N (vr) are pairwise disjoint and G has no isolated vertex, then γt(G) ≥ r.
2. If N [v1], . . . , N [vr] are pairwise disjoint, then γ(G) ≥ r.

3. If P ⊆ V (G) and every vertex of G has at most two neighbours in P , then γt(G) ≥ |P |/ 2.
Proof. (1) A total dominating set meets each N (vi), and these sets are disjoint. (2) is the same
argument for closed neighbourhoods. (3) If D is total dominating, every vertex of P has a neighbour
in D, so |P | ≤ ∑
x∈ D |N (x) ∩ P | ≤ 2|D|.
Lemma 3.2 (folklore). If G has no isolated vertex, then γt(G) ≤ 2γ(G).
Proof. Let D be a minimum dominating set and choose for each x ∈ D one neighbour x′. Every
vertex has a neighbour in D ∪ {x′ : x ∈ D}: a vertex outside D has one in D, and each x ∈ D is
adjacent to x′. This set has at most 2γ(G) vertices.

5 One-ended brooms and the size of the failure
Deﬁnition 5.1. For integers m ≥ 3 and k ≥ 1, the broom B(m, k ) consists of a path u0u1 · · ·um
and k further vertices ℓ1, . . . , ℓ k adjacent only to u0. It is a tree of order m + 1 + k.
Lemma 5.2. If m ≥ 3 and k ≥ max{2, m − 2}, then c(⟨N (L)∪ L⟩) = 2 and modemin(B(m, k )) = 1 ;
hence R(B(m, k )) = 2 + γ(B(m, k )).
Proof. The leaves are ℓ1, . . . , ℓ k, u m and the supports are u0 and um− 1, which are not adjacent
because m ≥ 3. So N (L) ∪ L induces a star centred at u0 and the edge um− 1um, with no edge
between them. The degree sequence has k + 1 ones, m − 1 twos and a single value k + 1 ≥ 3; as
k + 1 ≥ m − 1, degree 1 is the smallest mode.

6 Smallest counterexamples
Proposition 6.1 (computational). No tree of order at most 21 violates (1). Exactly four trees of
order 22 violate it, namely D(2, 8), D(3, 7), D(4, 6) and D(5, 5); each has γt = 8 and R = 7.
This statement was obtained by exhaustive computation over all 9 114 283 unlabelled trees
of orders 3 to 22, as described in Section 7. It is the only result of this paper that relies on
computation. The following table summarises the census for the largest orders; the full per-order

7 Reproducibility
The supplement wowii340-reproducibility.zip requires only Python 3.10 or later and no third-
party package. After extraction into an empty directory, the command
python -B -X utf8 reproduce340.py
veriﬁes the SHA-256 hashes listed in MANIFEST340.json, copies the programs into a fresh tempo-
rary directory without any stored results, runs them there, and compares the complete regenerated
JSON reports with the frozen reports in expected/. The default run replays the census up to
order 18 in a few minutes. The option --full replays orders 3 to 22; on a single core of a current
laptop this takes on the order of half an hour. The driver exits with a nonzero status on any mis-
match, and prints status: PASS otherwise. Assertions must be enabled; both programs refuse
optimised execution.
The certiﬁcate checker verify340.py. From explicit edge lists it recomputes γt and γ of
D(2, 8), D (3, 7), D (4, 6), D (5, 5), B(12, 10) and B(12, 11) by enumerating vertex subsets in increas-
ing cardinality, without any dynamic program. It checks the total dominating sets, dominating
sets and both packings of Theorems 4.3 and 5.3 for all 264 pairs 2 ≤ a ≤ b ≤ 24 with a + b ≥ 10
and for 10 ≤ k ≤ 40. It also checks the counting and covering bounds of Lemma 5.4 and the
inequality of Theorem 5.5 for 15 ≤ m ≤ 120 and k ∈ { m − 2, m − 1}. Its controls conﬁrm that
a single vertex never totally dominates, that repeated edges are rejected, and that D(4, 5) and
B(12, 9), just below the mode threshold, satisfy ( 1).
The census census340.py. Trees are generated by the constant-time free-tree successor algo-
rithm of Wright, Richmond, Odlyzko and McKay [ 11], which lists each isomorphism class exactly
once. The program checks completeness against the tree counts of OEIS A000055 [ 10] for every
order. It checks distinctness by computing, for every tree, the Aho–Hopcroft–Ullman canonical
code [ 1] of the tree rooted at its centre (the lexicographically smaller code at a bicentre), and
requiring the 64-bit BLAKE2b digests of these codes to be pairwise diﬀerent. The parameters
γt and γ are computed by two separately written linear-time tree dynamic programs: one with
per-vertex state arrays, the other by min-plus folding of child state vectors. On every tree of order
at most 11 both are compared with exhaustive subset enumeration. For orders up to 16 the two
programs are compared on every tree. For larger orders the second program is evaluated whenever
the ﬁrst gives γt > c (⟨N (L) ∪ L⟩) + mode min, which is necessary for a violation because γ ≥ 1;
the programs must agree on all such trees. The census record lists, for each order, the number of
trees, the minimum slack and every violation together with its parent array.

8 Source, status and priority
The statement and the linked deﬁnitions were taken from the Internet Archive capture of 23 July
2026 of the original WOWII pages [ 5]. The supplement retains the compressed capture bytes with
their SHA-256 hashes, the extracted row 340, and the extracted deﬁnitions 45, 46, 49, 94 and 100,
and the replay re-extracts them from the stored bytes. On 17 October 2026 the original endpoint
redirected and then timed out. The archived row bears the status marker O. This is historical
evidence of open status, not a statement by the curator about the present.
We found no earlier resolution. The 2009 paper of DeLaviña, Larson, Pepper and Waller
[6] discusses other entries of the same block. A public worklog by Dubey [ 7] reports that entry
340 survives every tree of order at most 18, which agrees with Proposition 6.1. The archive of
Bhattacharjee [ 2] refutes Conjectures 319, 352, 358, 359 and 427 and does not mention 340. The
repository formal-conjectures [8] contains no formalisation of WOWII 340. A full-text scan on
17 October 2026 of all 244 issues and pull requests matching “WOWII”, “WrittenOnTheWallII”,
“Written on the Wall” or “Graﬃti.pc” found no mention of it; the repository was then at commit
c77fdd446d23. Targeted web searches found nothing further. These bounded searches do not
establish worldwide priority.
